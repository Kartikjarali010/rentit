A hyperlocal peer-to-peer marketplace where people rent idle household/hobby/trade items to neighbours. Platform earns 15–20% commission per transaction.

## Details

**Problem:** Most households own items used once or twice a year (drill, pressure washer, camping gear). Renters don't want to buy, owners want passive income from idle stuff.

**Who:**
- Supply: owners of idle items seeking passive income
- Demand: renters who need something short-term, don't want to buy

**Solution:**
1. Owner lists item (photos, price/day, availability, location radius)
2. Renter nearby discovers → sends request
3. Owner accepts → payment held in escrow
4. In-person exchange → item used → returned
5. Platform releases funds minus commission

**Revenue model:** 15–20% commission on each completed transaction. No listing fee at launch.

**Why now:** *(not yet articulated — needs a reason this is the right moment)*

**Stage:** Ideation / validation — no product, no team, no budget, no city chosen

## Board Conditions (must resolve before building)

- [ ] **Damage & trust policy designed end-to-end** — who is liable, evidence required, what platform covers, resolution timeline. Must exist before any design begins.
- [ ] **Cold-start plan names a specific first neighbourhood + path to 50 listings** — not a city, a walkable radius. Named cohort, named outreach tactic, stated cost per listing.
- [ ] **One item category + one geography chosen, defended** — not "tools in a city", one type of item in one dense area.
- [ ] **Competitive mapping done** — Fat Llama, Loanables, local Facebook/NextDoor rental behaviour. *(→ run `/founder-competitors`)*
- [ ] **A "secret" or structural advantage identified** — one thing not replicable from a job board and a Stripe account.
- [ ] **Handoff experience prototyped + tested** — 5 real exchanges (even informal) before any backend is built.

## Strongest version (board thesis)
Not a consumer app. A **hyperlocal tool/equipment rental network for a specific trade community** — small contractors, makers, hobbyists who already borrow from each other informally. Platform formalises the trust layer they're missing + adds discoverability. Small, ownable first market. Natural expansion path.

> This is a pivot from the original pitch (general consumer marketplace). Founder should decide if it's interesting before running more of the founder pack.

---

## Risks (consensus across all 3 board members)
1. **Cold-start has no answer** — founding problem, not a marketing problem
2. **Damage & trust model is the product, not a feature** — rational owner won't list a $400 pressure washer with no recourse

## Additional risks (single members)
- No proprietary advantage named — cloneable in weeks
- 8-10 experience seams undesigned (handoff, owner accepts delay, etc.)
- Supply quality uncontrolled — early word-of-mouth set by worst interactions

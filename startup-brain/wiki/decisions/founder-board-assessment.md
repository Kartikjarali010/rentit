Full founder board assessment of the RentIt idea. Run via `/founder-board` skill. 3 lenses: Offers, Monopoly, Product.

## Verdict
**3× FUND IF | Average score: 3.7/10**

No PASS. No FUND. Real idea, real pain, three structural holes before the first user touches the product.

---

## Offers Lens (4/10 — FUND IF)

**Strengths:**
- Pain is real and recurring — most households own dozens of items used 1-2x/year
- Dream outcome scores well for both sides (renter: get thing fast, pay little; owner: passive income from idle items)
- Effort ask on renter is structurally low
- Commission-only pricing is correct at this stage — no listing fee removes first barrier for supply

**What would kill it:**
1. **Perceived likelihood of success near zero without density** — value equation's most dangerous lever
2. Supply quality uncontrolled — owners set own prices/availability; early word-of-mouth set by worst interactions
3. *(see conditions checklist in idea page)*

---

## Monopoly Lens (3/10 — FUND IF)

**Strengths:**
- Hyperlocal geography = natural ceiling → right shape for monopoly-first strategy; first city can be owned before anyone notices
- Transaction data creates moat (location, item-type, pricing, reliability) — trust history not replicable by clones
- Commission model scales with density without renegotiation

**What would kill it:**
1. **Cold-start has no stated solution — it's existential** — zero listings = worthless to renters; zero renters = worthless to owners
2. **No proprietary advantage** — described product cloneable in weeks; competes on execution alone, which is fragile
3. **Damage/trust/insurance unsolved** — a competitor who solves the trust layer cleanly while this platform runs dispute resolution by email *is* the better product

**Questions the board would ask:**
- What is the go-to-market wedge? Named cohort + tactic for first 50 listings?
- What is the secret? What does this founder know that a copycat can't replicate from a job board?
- What is the first market? Not a city — a specific segment.
- What stops Airbnb/Facebook Marketplace at 12 months?

---

## Product Lens (4/10 — FUND IF)

**Strengths:**
- Core transaction describable in one sentence — no explanation problem
- Escrow-held payment is the right structural choice — removes awkward cash moment, natural dispute hold point
- In-person handoff can be a trust touchpoint (lightweight checklist, photo capture, condition-confirmation) if designed well

**What would kill it:**
1. **Damage/disputes/no-shows have no stated resolution** — platform is the arbiter; without a documented policy, product can't ship to real users
2. **8-10 experience seams undesigned** — "owner accepts" step alone introduces indeterminate wait that kills conversion for time-sensitive needs
3. **Nothing that makes this feel like one product** — commission on P2P rentals is a revenue model, not a product vision; without a clear point of view, every feature decision made by committee

**Questions the board would ask:**
- Walk through the moment an item comes back damaged — what does each side see? Who decides?
- Optimising for owner listing or renter finding? Can't do both brilliantly with no team.
- Have you done the in-person handoff yourself? What felt broken?
- What is the one item category you're launching with?

## Risks all three members raised

These are the dangers the board agrees on. They go first.

**1. The cold-start problem has no answer, and it is the founding problem.**
Every member raised this independently. A two-sided marketplace with no listings is worthless to renters; a marketplace with no renters is worthless to owners. The brief names this as unknown. That is honest, but it cannot stay unknown. Without a named first neighbourhood, a named first cohort of 50 owners, and a tactic to reach them, every other analysis is premature. This is not a marketing question — it is the question.

**2. The damage and trust model is the product, not a feature.**
All three members called this out as the most dangerous invisible gap. No deposit, no insurance, no dispute resolution — these are not v2 items. A rational owner does not list a $400 pressure washer for $15/day with no recourse if it comes back broken. The owner-side value proposition does not exist until this is designed. The renter-side experience falls apart the first time something goes wrong. The resolution flow must be written before the first screen is designed.

---

## Additional risks (single members)

- **No proprietary advantage named** (Monopoly lens): the described product can be cloned in weeks. What does the founder know or control that a copycat cannot replicate from a job board and a Stripe account?
- **Experience seams are undesigned** (Product lens): 8–10 discrete handoff moments between renter, owner, platform, and payment system — none designed. The "owner accepts" delay alone kills conversion for time-sensitive needs.
- **Supply quality is uncontrolled** (Offers lens): owners set their own prices and decide their own availability. Early word-of-mouth is set by the worst interactions, not the average.

## The conditions checklist

The board's FUND IF conditions, merged and deduplicated. The rest of the founder pack will test most of these:

- [ ] **Damage and trust policy designed end-to-end.** Who is liable for damage, what evidence is required, what the platform covers, what it does not, resolution timeline. Must exist before any user-facing design begins. *(→ `founder-cfo` models the unit economics once the mechanism is chosen)*
- [ ] **Cold-start plan names a specific first neighbourhood and a specific path to 50 listings.** Not a city — a walkable or driveable radius. Named cohort of owners, named outreach tactic, stated cost per listing.
- [ ] **One item category and one geography chosen for launch, with a reason.** "Tools in a city" is not specific enough. One type of item, one dense area, defended.
- [ ] **Competitive mapping done before the offer is finalised.** Fat Llama, Loanables, local Facebook/NextDoor rental behaviour. *(→ `/founder-competitors` runs this)*
- [ ] **A "secret" or structural advantage identified.** One thing the founder knows or controls that is not replicable from a job board and a Stripe account — a community, a distribution channel, a supply pocket, domain expertise.
- [ ] **Handoff experience prototyped and tested with real people.** Five real exchanges (even informal) before any backend is built. The finding from those tests opens the next conversation.

## The strongest version of this business

The board does not see a consumer app as the strongest version. It sees a **hyperlocal tool and equipment rental network for a specific trade community** — not the general public. Here is the logic: the cold-start problem dissolves if supply and demand already know each other. A dense community of small contractors, makers, or hobbyists in one city who already own and borrow from each other informally is the supply. The platform formalises the trust layer they are already missing (payment safety, item condition records, dispute resolution) and adds discoverability beyond their immediate network. That is a small, ownable first market with a natural expansion path outward. It is different from what the founder pitched — a general consumer marketplace — but it is the version the board can see surviving long enough to compound.

The founder should decide whether that pivot is interesting before running the rest of the pack.

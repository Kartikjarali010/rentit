# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

**The cold-start constraint is real, not invented.** A hyperlocal rental marketplace has a natural geographic ceiling on the initial TAM. That is not a weakness here — it is the right shape for a monopoly-first strategy. The first city can be owned before anyone notices. Done right, the platform that owns one dense neighbourhood first has the liquidity advantage every later entrant has to buy back.

**Transaction structure creates potential data moats.** Every completed rental generates location, item-type, pricing, and reliability data about both parties. After enough volume, that history becomes a trust asset that a new entrant cannot replicate by copying the UI. A renter who has a 4.9 rating across 40 transactions will not abandon that for a blank slate on a competing platform — this is the seed of a network-effect moat, even if it has not been planted yet.

**The commission model scales with density.** Unlike subscription models, it does not need to be renegotiated as the platform grows. If density in one area drives transaction frequency up, revenue grows without changing the product or the pricing page.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)

**1. The cold-start problem has no stated solution, and it is existential.**
A two-sided marketplace with zero listings is worthless to renters; a marketplace with zero renters is worthless to owners. The brief names this openly: how the first supply and demand will be acquired is unknown. This is not a gap to fill later — it is the founding problem. Without a specific, credible answer, every other analysis is premature. The Monopoly lens asks how the first 100 customers arrive and what each costs. Neither is answered. This gap alone justifies a conditional vote.

**2. No proprietary advantage has been identified.**
The engineering question is unanswered. The brief describes a standard commission marketplace: list, discover, pay, exchange, return. That is a workflow any developer can replicate in weeks. The question is not whether this can be built — it can — but whether there is anything here that is 10x better on a dimension that matters to the user. No technology, no data asset, no proprietary process, and no distribution channel has been named. The business in its current description competes on execution alone, which is fragile.

**3. Damage, trust, and insurance are unsolved, and they are the product.**
A renter who breaks a lawnmower, an owner who refuses to return a deposit, a no-show on exchange day — these are not edge cases to handle in v2. They are the core reason peer-to-peer rental platforms fail to reach density. The Monopoly lens asks what stops a copycat in month six: a competitor who solves the trust layer cleanly while this platform is still running dispute resolution by email is not a copycat, it is the better product. The brief notes this is unknown. It needs to be known before launch, not after.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. **What is the go-to-market wedge?** Which specific, reachable group of owners (by item type, neighbourhood, platform, or community) will supply the first 50 listings, and what does it cost to acquire each one? Not a channel — a named cohort and a tactic.

2. **What is the secret?** The Monopoly lens requires that a founder know something the market does not. What does this founder know — about a supply pocket, a trust mechanism, a distribution channel, or an underserved item category — that makes this more than a re-implementation of a known model?

3. **What is the trust and damage model?** How will item damage be priced, capped, or insured? Who absorbs a disputed transaction? The answer to this question determines whether the platform can grow past early adopters who tolerate ambiguity.

4. **What is the first market?** Not a city — a specific segment within a city. Which item category, in which neighbourhood, with which community of owners, can be owned at 80%+ supply coverage before a competitor arrives?

5. **What stops Airbnb, Facebook Marketplace, or a well-funded clone from entering once traction is visible?** Name the moat that will exist at 12 months, not the one that might exist at five years.

   Open question for the CFO skill: What is the blended CAC on both sides at target commission, and what transaction frequency is required to recover it? This number does not exist yet — `founder-cfo` answers it.

   Open question for the competitor skill: Who is operating in adjacent geographies or item categories today, what traction do they have, and what has stopped them from scaling? `founder-competitors` answers it.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **A credible cold-start plan.** A named first market (item category + geography + owner cohort), a specific acquisition tactic for the first 50 owners, and a stated cost per listing. Not a marketing strategy — a literal plan for the first week of supply.

2. **An identified secret or structural advantage.** One thing the founder knows or controls that is not replicable from a job posting and a Stripe account. This can be a distribution relationship, a community with excess inventory, proprietary trust infrastructure, or domain expertise in a specific item category — but it must exist and be named.

3. **A resolved trust and damage model.** A stated policy on item damage (who pays, how disputes are decided, whether escrow or insurance is used) before the first transaction, not after the first dispute.

These three conditions are pre-launch requirements, not post-seed milestones. A founder who can answer them has a business. A founder who cannot has a hypothesis — a reasonable one, but not yet fund-able under this lens.
